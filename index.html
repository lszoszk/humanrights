    The Universal Declaration of Human Rights and the two Covenants were drafted at the United Nations between 1946 and 1966, and the drafting records run to more than eighteen thousand pages. To find who said what, and why a provision reads as it does, one reads them page by page. UN Travaux Préparatoires, an independent resource built from the UN's own documents, divides them into speeches, each with its speaker, delegation, date, article and page.

    Search opens every result in its record, at its page, with a ready citation and a link to the UN copy. Each article can be followed through the drafting: its text at every stage, the meetings that debated it, and every vote, by name where the record gives one. Ask takes a question in plain words and returns the passages closest to it in meaning, even when the delegates used other words.

    Summary records paraphrase the speakers, the text is machine-read, and about one article tag in twenty is wrong: the About page gives every figure, and every passage links to the scan. AI brings new risks, such as invented quotations, and two tools answer them.

    Check holds every citation and quotation in your draft against the record it names. Through the MCP server and the Claude skill, your AI assistant cites the records by symbol and page, instead of from memory. The UN document remains the authority, one click away from every speech.

Regional human rights courts

For now, HUDOC Researcher covers the European Court of Human Rights. Tools for two more regional courts are under way.

  • HUDOC Researcher

    Judgments of the European Court of Human Rights, paragraph by paragraph, with their citation network.

    • 20k+ judgments in English
    • 9,000+ only in French
    Open HUDOC Researcher
    Transcript of the HUDOC Researcher film

    Working with the tens of thousands of judgments of the European Court of Human Rights usually means a database search and then other tools (PDFs, spreadsheets, notes, reference managers) to organize the results by hand. HUDOC Researcher, an independent toolkit built on HUDOC data, brings that work into one place.

    Every judgment is split into the Court's own paragraphs, each labelled with the part of the judgment it belongs to. Search takes keywords, phrases and Boolean operators, as in HUDOC, and a side drawer shows the paragraph in its context, with a link to the same paragraph in HUDOC and a ready OSCOLA citation. The beta holds all the judgments in English (over 20,000) and the judgments HUDOC publishes only in French (over 9,000), the most-cited also in unofficial English translation.

    When you know only the facts, Semantic Search lets you describe the case in plain words (in any language), and its citation network shows which judgments the Court builds on. Statistics present the whole corpus by year, formation, State, Article and outcome. Research in the age of AI brings new risks, such as invented case names and quotations, and HUDOC Researcher answers them with three tools.

    Check compares every citation and quotation in your draft with the text of the judgment. Through the MCP server and the Claude skill, your own AI assistant quotes the Court verbatim, with the case, application number and paragraph, instead of paraphrasing from memory. HUDOC remains the authoritative source, and HUDOC Researcher is built to keep it one click away from every paragraph.

Use with your AI assistant

Four of the tools also work in Claude, ChatGPT and other assistants that support the Model Context Protocol (MCP). The assistant then quotes the documents verbatim, with symbol and paragraph, and Check lets you compare its answer with the source.

Why you can rely on it

  1. No answer is generated

    Every result is the verbatim text of the document, with its UN symbol and paragraph (or case name and §).

  2. The paragraph is the unit

    One click copies a paragraph, a speech, a vote or a recommendation with its full citation.

  3. One click from the source

    Each result links to the official record (UN documents, OHCHR databases or HUDOC).
